# Primitive Minini ECU

`custom_motor_control.lua` owns the Interpreter, TRC and MiTVS functions. The adapter applies their final commands. Civetta's policy and installed vehicles are separate.

Interpreter returns a normalized acceleration request: accelerator 0..1, or negative brake 0..-1 whenever brake is pressed. These are pedal demand units, not m/s². Accelerator drives the selected direction; brake slows the current travel direction. The physical parking brake remains available.

TRC receives that request, wheel speed/RPM, local rolling ground speed, motor torque/capacity and chassis travel speed. Steering and rotation-center targets are excluded from its input. Each wheel's displayed slip level is absolute longitudinal slip divided by its learned peak-slip target: 0% is zero longitudinal slip, 100% is the estimated peak and values above 100% are overshoot.

Peak search starts at 10% slip. It probes targets 1.2 percentage points above and below the current estimate on a 0.6 s cycle, compares settled transmitted torque (`motor torque - wheel inertia * wheel angular acceleration`), and tracks an observed local force/slip curve to reject misleading probe phases during recovery. After 1.3 s of initial observation, it sweeps 3..29% target slip in steps of two percentage points, holding each for 0.3 s. This 4.2 s search crosses force valleys without reversing at the first downturn and selects the strongest measured slip bin. Local probes then refine that result; a new sweep starts after 12 s. The target adapts within 3..30% slip; local curve samples expire after 3 s. Search scores measured slip rather than commanded slip, and severe runaway recovery remains active. Driving and braking retain separate peak estimates. The slip servo uses transmitted-torque feedforward plus wheel-speed feedback and bounded torque changes. It increases drive torque when a wheel is too slow; it only treats overspeed as drive wheelspin, and underspeed as braking lock-up. Severe runaway slip permits faster recovery. There is no exponential power cut at the first 100% crossing.

These are primitive estimates: suspension disturbances, inertia errors and rolling resistance can bias them. They do not measure lateral tire saturation or guarantee the tire's true friction peak. Offline dynamic-plant tests use three distinct rising/falling force curves and retain at least 95% of available traction after settling. A two-peak curve with a deep valley verifies recovery to the stronger peak with over 90% traction; disabling the broad sweep leaves that case around 54%. This selects the strongest sampled peak in the bounded range, rather than guaranteeing a global optimum under every changing surface. This does not establish real-game performance.

TRC maintains per-wheel torque allowances, starting from zero and building through the same feedback at every road speed. It reduces them on overshoot and recovers them below peak; zero allowance is permitted during runaway recovery. Slip uses actual rolling ground speed, with only a 0.05 m/s denominator floor to avoid division by zero at rest (the previous 2 m/s floor is removed). A motor-capacity-scaled slip-error term restores torque even when speed-dependent feedforward is weak. Dynamic launch tests at 1, 10, 30 and 100 km/h verify at least 0.85 G during 0.5..1.0 s and less than 0.05 G spread on the same modeled tire curve. It supplies four baseline Nm commands, slip levels and positive/negative allowances to MiTVS. There is no separate traction-control stage after MiTVS.

The ECU computes ERC from the four smoothed IMUs. The display mod consumes that same result. WRC remains 10 m to the steering side, WRS remains 30 km/h. MiTVS activates only with valid ERC/WRC and ERC distance strictly below 15 m. It subtracts the wanted and estimated center vectors and speeds. The desired center is converted to angular rate at the wanted speed; angular-rate error requests a corrective moment and WRS-minus-ERS requests longitudinal force. Initial gains are 6/s for angular-rate feedback and 0.6/s for speed feedback. Yaw inertia is approximated from mass, wheelbase and track.

An active-set weighted allocator distributes added Nm within the motor/TRC bounds. Overslipping wheels receive less weight. A saturated side hands its work to other wheels. When only one side remains, yaw correction takes priority over exact longitudinal-force matching. Torque changes may cross zero, so assistance can apply either drive or regeneration on any wheel. During active MiTVS, the fixed WRS target can also request torque with both pedals released. This temporary target is independent of pedal demand.

MiTVS Debug shows WRC, ERC, signed lateral RC error, WRS, ERS and speed error. Shares sum to 100% of absolute MiTVS-added Nm when correction exists, or all show 0% when it does not. Green/red bars and motor percentages describe only `final Nm - TRC baseline Nm`; they never show total final motor output. A reversal can change torque by more than one motor's peak, so the displayed correction percentage may exceed 100% while the bar stops at its graphical limit. TRC percentages are not clamped at 100%.

The standalone test bench still bypasses this policy while a prescribed-torque test is running. Offline checks cover pedal priority, traction overshoot, peak adaptation, the 15 m gate, correction signs, saturation redistribution, reverse travel, telemetry and IMU/RC fusion. Handling needs validation in BeamNG.
